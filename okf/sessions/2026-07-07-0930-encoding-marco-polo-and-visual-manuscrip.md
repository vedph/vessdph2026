# Abstract

Marco Polo was born in Venice in 1254, a city that was then a powerful maritime republic and a gateway between East and West. His famous book, Il Milione, written after his return from Asia, describes the extraordinary lands he explored along the Silk Road. Through his vivid accounts of China and the court of Kublai Khan, Venice became symbolically connected to the wonders of the East. The work reflects the curiosity, commercial ambition, and global vision that characterized Venetian society. Even today, Marco Polo represents the adventurous spirit of Venice and its historic role as a bridge between cultures. Following a concise overview of the digital projects on Marco Polo currently underway in Venice, the activity proposed for this session will consist in the encoding of a short excerpt from Il Milione, drawn from Bodley MS 264, in accordance with the XML-TEI standard. Particular attention will be devoted to the subsequent visualization of the encoded text in conjunction with the manuscript’s iconographic apparatus, which is unparalleled in its scope and conception.

In the afternoon, a visit to the State Archives of Venice is scheduled, where medieval archival documents relating to the Polo family (legal and administrative records) are preserved. These materials are invaluable for reconstructing the biographical, social, and legal dimensions of the family, as well as Marco Polo’s presence in the city following his return from China.

# About the speaker

Marina Buzzoni is Full Professor of Germanic Philology and Linguistics at Ca’ Foscari University of Venice and President of AIUCD, the Italian Association for Digital Humanities and Digital Culture. Her research combines Germanic philology, textual criticism, manuscript studies, and digital scholarly editing, with a particular interest in the theory and practice of digital editions.

Chiara De Bastiani is a researcher in Germanic Philology and Linguistics at Ca’ Foscari University of Venice. Her research interests include the history of Germanic languages, historical and comparative linguistics, corpus linguistics, digital philology, and digital scholarly editions.

Paola Peratello is affiliated with both Ca’ Foscari University of Venice and the École nationale des chartes–PSL in Paris. At Ca’ Foscari, she is an adjunct professor and subject expert in Germanic Philology and Linguistics; in Paris, she is a member of the Centre Jean-Mabillon within the CultureLab research programme. Her work focuses on Nordic manuscript traditions, runology, palaeography and codicology, digital philology, TEI encoding, digital scholarly editions, multispectral imaging, and handwritten text recognition.
