# Abstract

This workshop offers a hands-on introduction to the Lettere familiari a diversi (1580) by Veronica Franco, a Venetian poet and intellectual of the Renaissance, widely known for her role as a cortigiana onesta and for her active participation in contemporary literary and cultural debates. Far from being a simple collection of private correspondence, the Letters constitute a carefully crafted literary work in which Franco constructs her authorial voice and intervenes in moral, social, and political discussions of her time. Particular attention will be devoted to the letter addressed to Henry III of France, a text that exemplifies Franco’s rhetorical skill and her strategic engagement with power, patronage, and political representation.

# About the speaker

Tiziana Mancinelli is a researcher at the Italian Institute of Germanic Studies (Istituto Italiano di Studi Germanici) and a collaborator of the Venice Centre for Digital and Public Humanities (VeDPH) at Ca’ Foscari University of Venice. Her research focuses on digital textual scholarship, textual modelling, TEI encoding, digital scholarly editions, and the sustainable production and publication of digital cultural heritage. She is involved in DiScEPT, a research project developing an open platform for digital scholarly editions and aligned translations.

Daniele Fusi is a researcher at the Stuttgart Research Centre for Text Studies, an adjunct lecturer at Ca’ Foscari University of Venice, and a collaborator of the Venice Centre for Digital and Public Humanities (VeDPH). A classical philologist and software developer, he works on digital textual scholarship, historical linguistics, metrics, digital lexicography, and the modelling of complex textual and cultural data. He is the creator of Cadmus, an open-source framework for building collaborative research resources, and currently contributes to the digital edition of Goethe’s Venetian Epigrams.

Paolo Monella is a tenure-track researcher in Latin and Digital Humanities at Kore University of Enna and an associate member of the Venice Centre for Digital and Public Humanities (VeDPH). His research combines Latin philology, digital scholarly editing, textual modelling, digital graphematics, semantic annotation, and research infrastructures. He is also a member of the PAGES ERC project at Sapienza University of Rome and serves as Secretary and a member of the Executive Board of AIUCD, the Italian Association for Digital Humanities and Digital Culture.
