# Abstract

AI-generated imagery looks like a sudden novelty, but it belongs to a much longer history connecting machine learning, statistics, and visual culture. The same logic behind today's generative systems, learning patterns from examples rather than following fixed rules, already appears in earlier, pre-digital attempts to aggregate collective preference into a single image, which produced strikingly generic, homogenized results. At the center of this history is latent space: the compressed representation in which generative models organize the essential features of their training data. Latent space is not just a technical mechanism. It works as a cultural infrastructure, reshaping inherited categories like authorship, style, and referent. It also changes what an archive can be. Instead of preserving original documents, latent space dissolves them into statistical distributions, so that only variations, never originals, can be retrieved. Contemporary art and curatorial theory have started to grapple with this shift, and with the biases it carries from the data it comes from.
